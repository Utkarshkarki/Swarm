DOCUMENT_ID: gst_real_estate

PART I — GST APPLICABILITY ON REAL ESTATE TRANSACTIONS
=============================================================================

CLAUSE 1 — SALE OF LAND EXEMPT FROM GST

Schedule III of the CGST Act, 2017 explicitly excludes sale of land from the
definition of "supply". Therefore, sale of land is EXEMPT from GST entirely.
Land sale transactions do not attract any GST liability on either party.

CLAUSE 2 — SALE OF COMPLETED PROPERTY EXEMPT FROM GST

Sale of a building or apartment is exempt from GST if the sale takes place
AFTER the issuance of Completion Certificate (CC) or after first occupancy,
whichever is earlier. Post-CC transactions are treated as sale of immovable
property and are outside the scope of GST.
CRITICAL: Buyers must verify that the CC has been issued before purchase to
ensure no GST liability arises on the transaction.

CLAUSE 3 — UNDER-CONSTRUCTION PROPERTY — GST APPLICABLE

For under-construction apartments and buildings where the agreement for sale is
executed before the Completion Certificate or Occupancy Certificate is issued,
GST is applicable on the supply of construction service. The effective GST rates
are:

(a) AFFORDABLE HOUSING: Effective GST rate of 1% (without Input Tax Credit)
    Definition of affordable housing for GST: residential apartments with
    carpet area up to 60 sq. metres in metropolitan cities (Delhi, NCR, Mumbai,
    Kolkata, Chennai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad) and 90 sq. metres in other cities,
    AND the gross amount charged is up to Rs. 45 lakhs.

(b) OTHER RESIDENTIAL PROPERTIES (NON-AFFORDABLE): Effective GST rate of 5%
    (without Input Tax Credit) for residential apartments not qualifying as
    affordable housing.

(c) COMMERCIAL PROPERTIES: Effective GST rate of 12% (with Input Tax Credit)
    for commercial properties, shops, and offices in real estate projects.

PART II — GST RATES — DETAILED BREAKDOWN
=============================================================================

CLAUSE 4 — RESIDENTIAL AFFORDABLE HOUSING RATE (1%)

Notification No. 03/2019-CT(Rate) dated 29.03.2019 prescribes effective GST rate
of 1% for supply of construction of affordable residential apartments by a promoter
in a Real Estate Project (REP) (other than RREP) commenced on or after 1st April,
2019. The promoter is not eligible to avail ITC on goods and services used in
supply of such services.

CLAUSE 5 — RESIDENTIAL NON-AFFORDABLE HOUSING RATE (5%)

Notification No. 03/2019-CT(Rate) dated 29.03.2019 prescribes effective GST rate
of 5% for supply of construction services of non-affordable residential apartments.
The promoter shall not be eligible to avail ITC on goods and services used in the
supply of such services.
NOTE: The restriction of ITC was introduced to ensure that the builder does not
claim credit on materials while passing GST liability to buyers.

CLAUSE 6 — COMMERCIAL PROPERTY RATE (12% WITH ITC)

For commercial or mixed-use projects, the supply of construction services is
taxable at 12% GST, and the promoter is eligible to avail Input Tax Credit on
goods and services used for such supply.

CLAUSE 7 — COMPOSITE SUPPLY IN MIXED PROJECTS

In Residential Real Estate Projects (RREP) where the carpet area of commercial
apartments is not more than 15% of the total carpet area of all apartments in
the project, the entire project is treated as residential for GST rate purposes.
All apartments in such project shall be taxed at 1% (affordable) or 5%
(non-affordable) rates without ITC.

=============================================================================
PART III — INPUT TAX CREDIT (ITC) RULES
=============================================================================

CLAUSE 8 — REVERSAL OF ITC ON PROJECTS STARTED BEFORE APRIL 2019

For ongoing projects (commenced before 01.04.2019) where the promoter opted for
the new rate without ITC, ITC proportionate to the ratio of unconstructed
flats to total flats must be reversed as per the prescribed formula under
Rule 42 of CGST Rules.

CLAUSE 9 — ITC NOT AVAILABLE TO BUYERS

Buyers / allottees of residential apartments cannot claim ITC on the GST paid
on under-construction apartments. GST paid forms part of the cost of acquisition
and is only deductible as part of cost of improvement for capital gains purposes
on subsequent sale.

=============================================================================
PART IV — JOINT DEVELOPMENT AGREEMENTS (JDA)
=============================================================================

CLAUSE 10 — GST ON JDA — TAXABLE SUPPLY BY LANDOWNER

Under a Joint Development Agreement (JDA), the landowner transfers development
rights to the developer in exchange for constructed area (sharing arrangement).
GST is payable by the landowner on the transfer of Development Rights (TDR) or
Floor Space Index (FSI) to the developer.

CLAUSE 11 — TIME OF SUPPLY ON JDA

GST on TDR/FSI is payable by the landowner when the developer sells apartments
to buyers (i.e., on first sale to third parties). The rate applicable is based
on the type of apartment (1% affordable / 5% residential / 12% commercial).

CLAUSE 12 — REVERSE CHARGE MECHANISM (RCM) ON JDA

Under RCM, the promoter (developer) is liable to pay GST on the services received
from the landowner by way of transfer of development rights under a JDA.
This is covered under Notification No. 05/2019-CT(Rate) and is limited to the
portion of TDR attributable to the unsold inventory as on the date of issuance
of the Completion Certificate.

=============================================================================
PART V — STAMP DUTY AND GST — NON-DUPLICATION
=============================================================================

CLAUSE 13 — GST EXCLUDES STAMP DUTY

The value on which GST is charged must not include stamp duty and registration
charges as these are charges levied by the State Government and are outside the
purview of GST. The Agreement for Sale value for GST purposes is exclusive of
stamp duty and registration fees.

=============================================================================
PART VI — LONG TERM CAPITAL GAINS (LTCG) — INTERSECTION WITH GST
=============================================================================

CLAUSE 14 — GST PAID AS COST OF ACQUISITION FOR CAPITAL GAINS

GST paid on under-construction property forms part of the cost of acquisition or
cost of improvement for the purpose of computation of capital gains on subsequent
sale of the property, subject to the buyer not being eligible to claim ITC.

CLAUSE 15 — HOLDING PERIOD FOR LTCG — REAL ESTATE

Real estate assets are treated as long-term capital assets if held for more than
24 months (2 years). Long-term capital gains from sale of residential property
are taxed at 20% with indexation benefit under Section 112 of the Income Tax Act.
Under the Finance Act 2024 amendment, the LTCG rate on real estate was revised to
12.5% WITHOUT indexation benefit (with an option to choose the more beneficial
calculation for properties acquired before 23rd July 2024).

=============================================================================
PART VII — TDS ON REAL ESTATE — SECTION 194IA
=============================================================================

CLAUSE 16 — TDS UNDER SECTION 194IA

As per Section 194IA of the Income Tax Act, every buyer of immovable property
(other than rural agricultural land) is required to deduct TDS at the rate of
1% from the consideration payable to the seller, if the total consideration
exceeds Rs. 50 lakhs.

CLAUSE 17 — TDS APPLICABILITY ON UNDER-CONSTRUCTION PROPERTY

TDS under Section 194IA is applicable on each instalment paid by the buyer to the
builder in an under-construction property transaction, to the extent such
instalments together form part of consideration exceeding Rs. 50 lakhs.

CLAUSE 18 — ANNUAL INFORMATION RETURN (AIS)

All property transactions above Rs. 30 lakhs must be reported by the Registrar
of Properties to the Income Tax department. Buyers and sellers should ensure
that the transaction value reported in AIS matches the actual Agreement to Sale
value to avoid scrutiny under Section 148A of the Income Tax Act.

=============================================================================
PART VIII — GST REGISTRATION OBLIGATION FOR BUILDERS
=============================================================================

CLAUSE 19 — MANDATORY GST REGISTRATION FOR PROMOTERS

Every promoter / builder who supplies construction services (under-construction
apartments) is required to obtain GST registration regardless of the turnover
threshold, as per the provisions of the CGST Act, 2017 read with the Real Estate
specific notifications. Failure to register and collect GST constitutes an offence
under Section 122 of the CGST Act and attracts a penalty equal to 100% of the
tax due.

CLAUSE 20 — GST ON ADVANCE PAYMENT BY BUYER

GST liability arises at the time of receipt of advance (booking amount) by the
promoter from the buyer, as per Section 12 of the CGST Act (time of supply for
services). The promoter must issue a Receipt Voucher for advances received and
must discharge GST thereon within the prescribed due date.
