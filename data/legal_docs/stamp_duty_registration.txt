PART I — OVERVIEW OF STAMP DUTY IN INDIA
=============================================================================

CLAUSE 1 — LEGAL BASIS FOR STAMP DUTY

Stamp duty on real estate transactions in India is governed by the Indian Stamp
Act, 1899 and the respective State Stamp Acts. Stamp duty is a State subject
under the Constitution of India (Entry 63 of the State List). Each state
prescribes its own rates, exemptions, and concessions. The Registration Act,
1908 governs the mandatory registration of property documents.

CLAUSE 2 — MANDATORY REGISTRATION OF PROPERTY DOCUMENTS

Under Section 17 of the Registration Act, 1908, the following instruments
MUST be compulsorily registered:
(a) Instruments of gift of immovable property;
(b) Non-testamentary instruments purporting to create, assign, or extinguish
    rights or interests in immovable property of value exceeding Rs. 100;
(c) Leases of immovable property for any term exceeding 11 months.
Failure to register renders such instruments inadmissible as evidence in court.

CLAUSE 3 — STAMP DUTY PAYABLE ON MARKET VALUE OR AGREEMENT VALUE

Stamp duty is payable on the higher of:
(a) The actual consideration (agreement value), OR
(b) The Ready Reckoner rate / Circle rate / Guidance value fixed by the
    State Government for the area in which the property is situated.
The state authority (District Registrar's Office) has the power to impound
documents that are insufficiently stamped and levy deficit stamp duty + penalty.

PART II — STATE-WISE STAMP DUTY RATES (RESIDENTIAL PROPERTY)
=============================================================================

CLAUSE 4 — MAHARASHTRA STAMP DUTY RATES

Maharashtra (including Mumbai):
Stamp Duty: 5% of property value for properties in Municipal Corporation areas.
Additional Metro Cess (Mumbai): 1% additional metro cess on stamp duty value.
Local Body Tax (LBT): 1% for Municipal Corporation limits.
Total effective rate in Mumbai: 6% (5% stamp duty + 1% metro cess).
Registration Charges: 1% of property value, subject to a maximum of Rs. 30,000.
Women buyers: Concession of 1% stamp duty if property is solely in the name
of a woman (i.e., 4% stamp duty instead of 5% in Maharashtra).
NOTE: Maharashtra has an amnesty scheme (Abhay Yojana) for past under-stamped
documents which allows payment of deficit duty without penalty.

CLAUSE 5 — KARNATAKA STAMP DUTY RATES

Karnataka (including Bengaluru):
Stamp Duty: 5% of property value (Guidance Value).
BBMP Area Surcharge: 10% on stamp duty for BBMP jurisdiction properties.
Agricultural Land Cess: 3% on the stamp duty amount.
Total Effective Rate in BBMP areas: approximately 5.6–6%.
Registration Charges: 1% of property value.
Women Buyer Concession: 1% reduction (i.e., 4% stamp duty) for properties
solely in the name of a woman.
Government Guidance Value is reviewed and revised by the Sub-Registrar offices.

CLAUSE 6 — DELHI STAMP DUTY RATES

Delhi:
Stamp Duty (Men): 6% of circle rate or agreement value (whichever is higher).
Stamp Duty (Women): 4% of circle rate or agreement value.
Stamp Duty (Joint purchase, Man + Woman): 5%.
Registration Charges: 1% of the property value.
Delhi Circle Rates are divided into 8 categories (A to H) based on locality,
ranging from Rs. 7.74 lakh/sq. metre (Category A) to Rs. 23,280/sq. metre
(Category H).

CLAUSE 7 — TELANGANA STAMP DUTY RATES

Telangana (including Hyderabad):
Stamp Duty: 4% of property value.
Registration Fee: 0.5% of property value.
Transfer Duty: 1.5% of property value.
Total effective outflow: 6% (4% + 0.5% + 1.5%).
Market Value Assessment is based on the Basic Value (BV) registered by the
Inspector General of Registration (IGRS) Telangana on a locality-wise basis.

CLAUSE 8 — TAMIL NADU STAMP DUTY RATES

Tamil Nadu (including Chennai):
Stamp Duty: 7% of property value (guideline value).
Registration Charges: 4% of guideline value.
Total effective rate: 11%.
Tamil Nadu has the highest combined stamp duty + registration cost in India.
Special Reduced Rate: Agricultural land in rural areas is charged at a lower rate.

CLAUSE 9 — UTTAR PRADESH STAMP DUTY RATES

Uttar Pradesh (including Noida, Greater Noida, Lucknow):
Stamp Duty (Men): 7% of circle rate or agreement value.
Stamp Duty (Women): 6% of circle rate or agreement value (1% concession).
Registration Charges: 1% of property value.
Circle Rates in UP are fixed by the District Magistrate and vary by locality.

CLAUSE 10 — RAJASTHAN STAMP DUTY RATES

Rajasthan (including Jaipur):
Stamp Duty: 6% of property value.
Registration Charges: 1% of property value.
Women Buyer Concession: 5% stamp duty (1% reduction).

=============================================================================
PART III — EXEMPTIONS AND CONCESSIONS
=============================================================================

CLAUSE 11 — EXEMPTION FOR FIRST-TIME HOME BUYERS (SELECTED STATES)

Several states provide concessions or waivers for first-time home buyers:
Maharashtra: 1% concession on stamp duty if property value is below Rs. 45 lakh
and buyer is a first-time purchaser (applicable under certain state schemes).
Odisha: 100% exemption from stamp duty for women buying properties up to
Rs. 20 lakh in value.
UP: No specific first-time buyer concession, but the 1% women's concession applies.

CLAUSE 12 — AFFORDABLE HOUSING STAMP DUTY CONCESSION

Maharashtra — Properties registered under PMAY (Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana) or
state-notified affordable housing schemes:
Stamp Duty: 1% only (irrespective of location).
Registration Charges: Nil (waived for properties under PMAY).
Condition: Property must be in an approved affordable housing project with carpet
area not exceeding 30 sq. metres in Mumbai and 60 sq. metres elsewhere.

=============================================================================
PART IV — TRANSFER VIA POWER OF ATTORNEY
=============================================================================

CLAUSE 13 — STAMP DUTY ON POWER OF ATTORNEY TRANSFERS

Power of Attorney (PoA) transfers are frequently used to evade stamp duty and
capital gains in real estate. Courts have progressively restricted their use.
Supreme Court Ruling in Suraj Lamp & Industries vs. State of Haryana (2011):
Sale through General Power of Attorney (GPA) / Agreement to Sell / Will
(popularly called GPA-ATS-WILL transactions) does NOT confer title to the buyer.
Such transactions are NOT valid substitutes for registered sale deeds.
Stamp Duty: PoA given for consideration is now required to be stamped at rates
equivalent to a sale deed (i.e., full stamp duty on the market value) in most states.

CLAUSE 14 — GIFT DEED STAMP DUTY

Gift of immovable property to family members:
Maharashtra: 200 rupees (nominal) if the gift is between blood relatives
(father, mother, brother, sister, spouse, son, daughter).
Karnataka: Nil stamp duty for gifts between specified family members.
Delhi: 2% stamp duty for gifts to family members (reduced from full rates).
Gift of property to non-family members is charged at full stamp duty rates.
Gift deeds must be compulsorily registered under Section 17 of the Registration Act.

PART V — REGISTRATION PROCEDURE
=============================================================================

CLAUSE 15 — DOCUMENTS REQUIRED FOR PROPERTY REGISTRATION

The following documents are mandatorily required for registration of a sale deed:
(a) Original sale deed / conveyance deed (executed on stamp paper of appropriate value)
(b) Encumbrance Certificate (EC) for minimum 13 years (30 years preferred)
(c) Latest property tax paid receipt
(d) Possession letter from builder (for new properties)
(e) Occupancy Certificate / Completion Certificate (for completed projects)
(f) No Objection Certificate (NOC) from Housing Society (if applicable)
(g) PAN Card of both buyer and seller
(h) Aadhaar Card of both buyer and seller
(i) Two passport-sized photographs each of buyer and seller
(j) Property location plan (site plan / index plan)
(k) Original title documents chain (if resale)

CLAUSE 16 — ENCUMBRANCE CERTIFICATE — CRITICAL DUE DILIGENCE

An Encumbrance Certificate (EC) is issued by the Sub-Registrar's office and
contains a record of all transactions registered against the property during a
specified period. An EC confirms that the property is free from any monetary
and legal liabilities. Absence of entry in the EC does NOT guarantee clear title
as oral transactions, unregistered agreements, and Wills are not captured in EC.
Buyers must supplement EC verification with a title search through original
documents and a lawyer's opinion on title.

CLAUSE 17 — CIRCLE RATE vs. AGREEMENT VALUE — SECTION 43CA

Where the agreement value of a property transaction is lower than the circle rate,
Section 43CA (for builders) and Section 50C (for sellers) of the Income Tax Act
deem the circle rate to be the consideration for the purposes of computing income
or capital gains. This prevents under-valuation of transactions to evade taxes.
TOLERANCE LIMIT: If the difference between the agreement value and circle rate
is within 10% (Finance Act 2021 extended to 20% for transactions entered into
between 12.11.2020 and 30.06.2021), the actual consideration is accepted.
